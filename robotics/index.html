PRINTED WINDINGS

Why PCB motor?

Conventional wound coils are replaced by precision-patterned PCB conductors, enabling a thin, repeatable and highly integrated axial-flux architecture.

Fast manufacturing

PCB-patterned windings support design iteration and repeatable manufacturing.

Slim design

Planar windings and axial-flux architecture support thin packaging for compact joints.

High-torque design

Electromagnetic, current and thermal design are optimized toward application torque requirements.

Low-vibration design

Design addresses torque ripple and electromagnetic excitation for smooth rotation.

Robotics and collaborative robots are the primary focus, with potential extensions to HVAC, small fans, pumps, fluid-transfer systems and premium appliances.

Manufacturing efficiency, high torque and low vibration are development objectives. Comparative performance is evaluated through defined test conditions and measured results.

LOW INDUCTANCE

A fundamental challenge of PCB motors

Low phase inductance can increase current ripple and complicate control. Adding external inductors introduces components and volume, reducing the packaging advantage in a compact robotic joint.

  1. PCB stator
  2. Low phase inductance
  3. Higher current ripple
  4. External inductor
  5. Additional system volume

FLUXMOTION TECHNOLOGY

Integrated inductance technology

Addressing low inductance while preserving the thin and lightweight advantages of PCB motors. The technology uses PCB patterns and an integrated fastening structure with a magnetic-core function.

CONVENTIONAL PCB MOTOR

  1. PCB stator
  2. Low inductance
  3. Higher current ripple
  4. External inductor may be required

FLUXMOTION TECHNOLOGY

  1. PCB stator + integrated inductance structure
  2. Increased phase inductance
  3. Reduced current ripple
  4. More compact motor system

Diagram shows the technology principle. The extent of improvement is subject to validation for each design and operating condition.
